# Licensing Suite Builder

Suite Builder is proprietary software. Two uses are free under the Suite Builder Personal and
Educational License 1.0 (see `LICENSE`):

- **Educational use:** a student's study or coursework, or a teacher teaching a course with it.
- **Personal projects:** an individual testing their own project, made and kept for themselves,
  with no commercial use.

**Every other use needs a commercial license from the author.** That includes any use by or for:

- a business, at work, for an employer or a client, or as part of any paid work;
- a nonprofit, charity, association or cooperative, whether or not anyone is paid;
- a government, its agencies, military, police or security services, or anyone working for them;
- a product, service or internal tool that Suite Builder is built into, or a service that
  generates test suites for others.

## The suites it generates

**Using a suite needs no license, only ownership of the site it tests. Generating a suite needs a
license.** A suite is licensed separately from the tool, and more permissively: it belongs to the
owner of the site it tests, who may use, change and share it for any purpose, whoever generated it
(see `GENERATED-OUTPUT.md`). Every suite carries its own `LICENSE` naming the site.

The licenses above are for generating suites:

- The **free license** covers a site owner generating a suite for their own personal project, and
  a student or teacher generating one for a course (a suite of someone else's site may then be
  used by them only for that course).
- A **commercial license** covers every other generation: by a business for its own site, by an
  agency or contractor for a client's, or, under a **Partner license** (below), by a service
  provider for its customers.
- Generating a suite without a license that covers it breaks Suite Builder's license, but the suite
  still belongs to the site's owner.

## The Partner license: generating suites for your customers

A **Partner license** is the commercial license for a service provider (a consultancy, a QA or
testing firm, a systems integrator) that generates suites for its customers. The customer gets the
suite; the partner keeps the tool.

- **A written agreement sets the scope:** its term, which customers or how many suites or sites
  it covers, the fees, and how the partner reports what it generated.
- **The partner's own staff run the tool.** It is never installed for, handed to or run by a
  customer, and the partner may not sublicense it. A customer that wants to generate suites itself
  needs a license of its own.
- **Every suite is recorded.** For each suite it generates, the partner records the site, the
  customer and the date, and reports them as its agreement says. The author may check that record
  as the agreement allows.
- **The customer owns the suite**, as the owner of the site it tests (`GENERATED-OUTPUT.md`): to
  use, change and share for any purpose, for good, during the agreement and after it ends. The
  customer needs nothing from the author to keep using it.

### Generating suites without a Partner license

Generating a suite for a customer with no Partner license, after the agreement has ended, or
beyond its scope, is use of Suite Builder that no license allows:

- It is **an infringement of the author's copyright** by the company that generated the suite and
  by the people who ran the tool for it. For a partner, it is also **a breach of its agreement**,
  and grounds to end it.
- The author may require that company to take a license covering every such suite, at the fees the
  author sets at the time, as well as any other remedy the law gives.
- **The claim is against the company that generated the suite, never its customer.** The suite
  still belongs to the customer, as the site's owner.

## Trying it before buying

There is no free trial of the tool for organizations, since one generated suite is most of what a
license buys. Instead, companies can join the **partner pilot** ([PILOT.md](PILOT.md)): with the
site owner's written permission, the author builds a suite for the company's site, and the company
evaluates it for 60 days at no cost. The suite is the company's to keep either way; further suites,
and rebuilds as the site changes, need a commercial license.

## How a commercial license is granted

In writing, on terms agreed with the author, for the person or organization that will use it. The
author may decline any license request.
